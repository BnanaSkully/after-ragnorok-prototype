# Ragnarok Prototype

Turn-based 4X strategy prototype in Godot 4, written in GDScript.

## The game

Survivors of Ragnarök cross the void on Yggdrasil (the Crossing), then land on a small hex map and build, scheme and survive until the Doom arrives. Politics first; combat is auto-resolved.

## Game structure

- Phase 1: the Crossing (journey events) and the Landing (found your city). The Sun's Daughter (the new sun, unnamed in the myths) travels with the survivors and watches how each people behaves. She has a Favour score (0-10) toward each faction: Crossing choices that protect people raise it, abandoning them lowers it. Each AI faction's Favour is hidden and random (4-7; was 3-6, contested Sunna step 1). At the Landing she chooses to shelter with whichever faction she trusts most (ties random). The player doesn't choose her; she chooses
- Sheltering her gives +1 Food, +1 Industry a turn and +1 city defence (was 2/2/3: she is a prize for politics, not an economy; contested Sunna step 1), but she is what Sköll hunts
- Economy (standard 4X): a city starts at population 1 making 2 Food and 2 Industry a turn; each extra population adds +1 Food, +1 Industry and +1 city defence (POP_DEFENCE; Nathan's playtest: population's effect was unclear, so it now also defends and every message about it says what it gives; the top bar shows "Pop"). Everyone unloads 5 Industry from the ark at the Landing (LANDING_INDUSTRY), enough to build something on turn 1. City growth: when stored Food reaches the growth cost (10, then 15, 20...) and Food isn't falling, the city grows and the Food is used up. Build options, each a trade-off: army unit 5 Industry (eats 1 Food a turn), scout 3, outpost 6, and city buildings under "Build..." (buildings.gd; Nathan's playtest: Industry piled up with nothing to spend it on): walls 10 per level (+4 defence, 2 levels), Granary 8 (+2 Food a turn), Workshop 12 (+2 Industry a turn), Watchtower 6 (city sight +2, +1 defence), Forge 10 (new army units +1 Strength), Barracks 8 (+2 army supply). Each building once. AIs build them when they can't feed more armies. On turn 15 an AI holds about 8 Industry unspent (was 18), the test player 12 (was 35). City defence = garrison unit's Strength + walls + land + population above 1 (+1 with the Daughter). Numbers: GROWTH_BASE, GROWTH_STEP etc. at the top of game_state.gd
- Early game (turns 1-10, see "Early game" below): every faction starts with an army unit and a scout, explores through fog of war, searches ruins, founds outposts, and the player gets a small event every 2-3 turns. Goal: every early turn asks the player a decision
- Phase 2 (turns 1-40, code phase PLAYING): settle, scheme and survive Sköll; see "Middle and late game (Part 2)" below. He arrives on turn 14 through one of the rifts, chosen at random, and hunts the city that rift belongs to. Every standing city gets one rift, 4-7 hexes from it and at least 4 from every city (Rifts.place in rifts.gd, MIN_CITY_DISTANCE; was 3, and Nathan's playtest had him appear "right next to my city"). Rifts are placed on turn 2 and stay hidden until found; when he comes through one, that rift is revealed. They are PERMANENT (full rifts step 2, Nathan 22 Nov): never rolled again, so a rift you find stays on your map. A unit standing on the rift he picks doesn't stop him: he comes out on a free hex next to it and attacks that unit (Skoll.come_through_rift, exit_beside). He scents Sunna: her protector's rift is twice as likely, so he first hunts her city about 40% of the time (contested Sunna step 2); otherwise every city is equally likely. Once he is loose, AI units fall back to defend their cities
- The game ends on turn 40 (END_TURN) or earlier (see Endings below). The old "Phase 3: the Long Night" design (chain, rogue factions, warbands) was replaced by Nathan's Part 2 spec; parked ideas from it are under "Later"

## Middle and late game (Part 2): built (Nathan's spec after the Part 1 playtest)

Sunna (the Sun's Daughter, named by Nathan) is the prize; Sköll keeps coming back; the world shrinks as ash spreads. Target length about 40 turns. Numbers are placeholders, measured but not yet played.

- Sköll's cycles (skoll.gd): Strength 10 (STRENGTH). He walks 2 hexes a turn toward the city he hunts (SPEED; was 1, Nathan's playtest: "he isn't scary anymore"), attacks it the turn he reaches it, and goes round a unit rather than through it when both ways are as close. Zone of control never applies to him. Driven back, he vanishes into the void for 3 turns (RETURN_TURNS) and comes back through a rift (they don't move), 2 stronger (STRENGTH_PER_RETURN). Each time he comes through a rift, 8 hexes turn to ash (ASH_PER_CYCLE, GameState.spread_ash)
- Wounds (standard HP combat, see Unit health): while Sunna is hidden (sheltered or rogue) he can't be killed. A unit attacking him dies, but the damage it deals wounds him; wounds lower his Strength (Skoll.effective_strength) and heal 2 a turn (WOUND_HEAL); wounds of half his Strength drive him back. Each other army of yours (or an ally's) next to him adds +2 to an attack (WOLF_SUPPORT, GameState.wolf_attack). Hovering him shows his Strength and wounds; the line under the top bar shows wounded / mortal / in the void
- Who he hunts: the city of the rift he came through, until he hits Sunna's city; then he knows where she is (knows_her) and hunts whoever shelters her (Skoll.her_city). She is the prize and the target
- City HP (standard 4X sieges): a city has 20 HP (CITY_MAX_HP) and repairs 3 a turn (CITY_HEAL). Attacks (units in a war, and Sköll) damage it by the HP-combat formula against the city defence; Sköll hits twice as hard (CITY_DAMAGE 2.0). A unit's siege that takes it to 0 makes the city a tributary (it restarts at 10 HP). Sköll taking it to 0 tears it down: that faction is devoured; if Sunna was there he swallows her; then he is sated and goes back into the void. HP bar under every city; the tooltip predicts a siege
- Ash: spreads in from the map's edges, from ash already there and around the rift he used. Dead land: outposts on it are lost, ruins buried, counts as nobody's land; units can still walk on it; never on a city or the hexes next to it. Stops once Sunna has risen
- Sunna's requests (from turn 8, every 4 turns, REQUEST_FROM / REQUEST_GAP; turn_events.gd SUNNA_REQUESTS): her protector is asked for something (Industry, Food, a guard, grain for the neighbours). Grant: the cost, her Favour +1. Refuse: Favour -2. An AI holding her pays 5 Industry (AI_REQUEST_COST) for +1, or takes -2. Being fed only raises her Favour up to 7 (FED_FAVOUR_CAP): to reach 8 you must grant her requests
- Rising (from turn 20, RISE_TURN): if her protector's Favour is 8+ (RISE_FAVOUR), another people recognises her and her city has held against Sköll (see "Sunna, the contested sun" step 4) she rises as the new sun and stands with them: +3 Food and +3 Industry a turn (RISEN_*), Sköll becomes MORTAL but ENRAGED (Nathan's choice after "the game just ends with Sunna rising"): he keeps his Strength (no reset to 10; wounds cleared), knows where she is, runs 3 hexes a turn (RAGE_SPEED) at her city, and tearing that city down swallows her. Dawn still needs her city standing on turn 40. The ash stops. Mortal, an attack that brings his wounds to his full Strength kills him (only an attack kills him: a city that holds just drives him back)
- Swallowed: if he tears down the city sheltering her (or reaches her in the open) he swallows her: +8 Strength (SWALLOW_BONUS) but mortal; only a large combined force (WOLF_SUPPORT) can bring him down
- Endings (GameState.final_ending, devour, wolf_slain): your city torn down by Sköll: "Devoured" (you lose). Sköll has devoured 45% of factions (2 of 4, DEVOURED_SHARE): "Sköll wins". You kill him: "Slay the wolf" (an AI killing him: they win). Turn 40: "Dawn" if Sunna rose with you, "Dawn for the X" if she rose with an AI; otherwise Dominion for whoever holds the most land not under ash (a tie counts as yours). The top bar shows "Turn N/40" and your "Land N"
- Sköll at 2 hexes a turn (100 games each, before -> after): passive player devoured 14% -> 20%, his returns 2.3 -> 3.0, ash 14 -> 20 hexes; strong player (army_test) Sköll's attacks on your city 0.5 -> 1.1 a game, devoured 8% -> 16%, wolf slain 6% -> 9%
- Enraged after she rises (100 games, speed 2 -> plus rage): passive player Dawn 20% -> 22%, devoured 20% -> 19%, swallowed 0.02 -> 0.05 a game; strong player devoured 16% -> 12%, wolf slain 9% -> 4%
- Measured (tests/midgame_test.gd, a passive player; quick run of 60 games, before SPEED 2): Sköll returns about 2 times a game, ash 15 hexes, Sunna rises 0.5 a game (0.23 with you), swallowed 0.02, requests 2.2, games last 36 turns, factions devoured 0.35. Endings roughly: Dawn 23%, devoured 32%, an AI's Dawn 17%, an AI's Dominion 28%. Strong player (army_test): ends with about 7 armies, kills the wolf in about 8% of games, devoured under 2%
- Known gaps: the AIs don't seek out Sunna's requests or rising on purpose (they only answer requests); Sunna choosing a new protector when she rises is not built (she rises with whoever holds her); text is placeholder

## Sunna, the contested sun: built (design agreed with Nathan 22 Nov), tuned once by simulation

Goal: Sunna is a prize every people wants and a danger to whoever holds her, not a free economy bonus. Four steps, one commit and one 300-game measurement each (tests/midgame_test.gd, a passive player; main before: Dawn 18%, devoured 17%, you hold her on turn 10 23%).
1. DONE: numbers. Sheltered bonus +1 Food, +1 Industry, +1 defence (SunDaughter.FOOD/INDUSTRY/DEFENCE, was 2/2/3). AI Favour at the Landing 4-7 (RIVAL_FAVOUR_MIN/MAX, was 3-6). Measured: you hold her on turn 10 23% -> 11%; Dawn 18% -> 8%; devoured 17% -> 31% (her holder's city is weaker, so Sköll tears down more cities: factions devoured 0.19 -> 0.34 a game, swallowed 0.04 -> 0.10)
2. DONE: Sköll scents her. When he comes through a rift, the rift of the city sheltering her counts double (Skoll.pick_rift, SCENT_WEIGHT 2); not while she's rogue, risen or swallowed. Measured: his first arrival hunts her protector 43% of the time (fair share 25%; skoll_spawn_test); Dawn 8% -> 10%, devoured 31% -> 27%, swallowed 0.10 -> 0.14 a game
3. DONE: rival claims. Every people believes she is its own lost sun: Sól (Norse), Amaterasu (Japanese), Helios (Greek), Belenus (Celtic) (SunDaughter.CLAIM_NAMES, claim_name; the messages use them). Every 4 turns while you shelter her, each AI's Trust toward you -1, at most -4 a game from this (GameState.rival_claims, CLAIM_*; AIs keep no Trust toward each other, so this only applies when you hold her). Holding her is a reason for war: an AI with Trust under 2 whose army isn't weaker than yours (10% a turn, rival_consider_war), and an AI whose army isn't weaker than her AI holder's (5% a turn, ai_wars_turn); an AI at war with her holder marches on that city first (CLAIM_SIEGE_PULL). From turn 10 an AI may demand her in its offers (pop-up "Hand her over" / Refuse; 50% of its offers while she's worth at least -3 to it, CLAIM_VALUE_MIN): handing her over Trust +1, refusing -1. Making her holder a tributary carries her off to the overlord (make_tributary). Measured: AIs demand her 0.24 a game, wars on you over her 0.14, AI wars over her 0.73, carried off by a conquest under 0.01 (sieges are rare); you hold her on turn 10 13%, Dawn 10% -> 6%, devoured 27% -> 30%
4. DONE: rising needs three things (GameState.rise_missing, maybe_rise): her protector's Favour 8+; another people recognises her (RECOGNISE_TRUST: an AI with Trust 2+ toward you, or an oath; for an AI protector it is you who recognise it, its Trust 2+ with you or an oath with you, since AIs keep no Trust toward each other); and her protector's city has held against at least one Sköll attack, or its armies drove him back near it (Faction.skoll_held, counted in Skoll.attack_city and Skoll.wound). The line under the top bar shows what is missing from turn 14 ("to rise: Favour 7/8, recognition (Trust 2+ or an oath), her city to hold off Sköll"). Giving her away (trading her, or handing her to a people that claims her; hand_over_daughter): the receiver's Trust +3 (GIVEN_TRUST) and it accepts your next oath or deal (Diplomacy.owes_you: oath, open borders, a demand, peace; chance shows 100%). tests/contested_screenshot.gd shows the line and a claim pop-up. Measured: Dawn with you 6% -> under 1% (2 of 300), Sunna rises 0.30 -> 0.05 a game (any faction), devoured 33%; games where only "held vs Sköll" kept her from rising: 4% for you, 13% for anyone
- Where the whole design lands (main -> all 4 steps, 300 games, passive player): you hold her on turn 10 23% -> 10%; Dawn with you 18% -> under 1% (Nathan's target about 15%); devoured 17% -> 33%; AI Dawns 31% -> 4%; Dominion endings rise to take their place. Tuned once (below)
- Tuning (Nathan's pick, 22 Nov): driving Sköll back within 3 hexes of your city (Skoll.HELD_RANGE, in Skoll.wound) also counts as your city holding against him. Measured, 300 games each, before -> after: a player who chases her (tests/active_sunna.gd: grants every request, keeps an oath) Dawn 11% -> 15% (target about 15%), devoured 30% -> 26%; passive player (midgame_test) Dawn under 1% -> 3%, devoured 33% -> 30%; strong army player (army_test, 200 games) Dawn 2% -> 2%

## Rift Walkers: built (full rifts step 4, the simple version Nathan picked)

- Lore (Nathan, 23 Nov): the broods are Yggdrasil's rogue army. In game text they are "broods" ("A brood crawls out of the rifts you know: Yggdrasil's rogue army", "Broods on your land"); the code keeps the name RiftWalker. A separate creature, a skeleton horde that rises from the dead near a rift, is lore only: no model or rules yet
- rift_walker.gd (class RiftWalker: position, home rift, HP; not a Unit, belongs to no faction); rules in rifts.gd (Rifts.walkers, walkers_turn, walker_at, walkers_on_land; numbers WALKERS_PER_SEASON 1, MAX_WALKERS_PER_RIFT 2, WANDER_RANGE 3, WANDER_CHANCE 0.5, WALKER_FOOD 1, WALKER_HEAL 2) and fights in game_state.gd (attack_walker, walker_defence, expected_walker_combat, ai_walker_target)
- When the season begins, one crawls out of every rift onto a free hex beside it (at most 2 alive per rift). They STAY until killed (more each season; Claude's choice, Nathan can pick "go back when the season ends" instead). Each turn each may step to a free neighbour (50%), never more than 3 from its rift, never into a city or onto a rift (so Sköll can always use the rift)
- Never attack first. Block a hex like a unit (GameState.is_blocked; paths go round; no outposts on them; Sköll goes round them). Attacked: the usual HP fight (Strength 3, 10 HP, terrain defence), both sides take damage; killed, the attacker moves in if it may. Attacking one is never an act of war (war_target returns null). They hold no zone of control and heal 2 a turn when not fought
- Only effect: each Walker on a faction's land costs it 1 Food a turn (GameState.food_parts: "Rift Walkers on your land"). Messages: how many came out of rifts you know, and when one wanders onto your land
- AI: an army next to a Walker on its own land attacks it (unless hurt to 4 HP or less)
- On screen: 2D a dark knot of roots with a blue rim and an HP bar; 3D Nathan's brood model (assets/models/units/brood.glb, static, BROOD_SCALE 1.3, on a cold blue ring; view_3d.make_walker_figure; it faces the way it last moved) with a blue map pin; tooltip with HP, Strength, whose Food it eats and the predicted fight; its hex is marked orange for attack next to a selected unit. tests/walker_test.gd, tests/walker_screenshot.gd
- Measured (300 games): about 10 come out a game, 5-6 on the map at once, 2.8 killed; a passive player loses 3 Food a game to them, each AI 13. Passive player (midgame_test) devoured 35% -> 39%, Dawn with you 10 -> 10 of 300; strong player (army_test, 200 games) devoured 28 -> 37 (both about 1 standard error; watch it). Details in NOTES.md

## Early game: built (standard 4X, from Nathan's playtest "turns 1-10 are dead")

- Fog of war with memory, for every faction (GameState.can_see, Faction.explored): each faction sees CITY_SIGHT (3) around its city, OUTPOST_SIGHT (2) around outposts, 2 around army units and 3 around scouts. Hexes once seen stay explored (land, cities, outposts, ruins stay known; units, Sköll and the Daughter only while in sight). Never-seen hexes are black. During the Landing the ark sees the shape of the land and where you may land, but not cities. The AIs explore and choose ruins/outposts by their own fog (they still always know where a rogue Daughter is: unchanged)
- Scouts (unit.gd, Unit.Kind.SCOUT): 2 moves, Strength 1, sight 3, eat no Food, 3 Industry, at most 2 per faction. A scout keeps its selection while it has a move left. Starving never makes a scout desert. AI scouts head for the nearest ruins they've seen, then the nearest unexplored hex; they never attack
- Discoveries (discoveries.gd): 8 ruins hidden at least 4 hexes from every city (out of sight at the start), placed at the Landing. The first unit to step on ruins searches them: the player chooses Food +6, Industry +4 or the Daughter's Favour +1 (pop-up; if her Favour is already 10, old maps that reveal where another people landed instead); any option that would raise Favour above 10 is greyed out with the reason (GameState.option_problem); an AI takes Food if short of it, else Industry. Rifts (Sköll's, placed on turn 2) become visible to a faction when it sees them and stay shown for the rest of the game (Faction.known_rifts; rifts are permanent). No choice on finding a rift yet (sealing rifts would change Sköll: ask Nathan first)
- Outposts (GameState.outpost_spots, build_outpost): 6 Industry; on a hex you can see right now, within 5 of your city, at least 3 from every city and outpost, not on unsearched ruins, not on a rift you know of. Rifts and outposts never share a hex (Nathan's choice after seeing an outpost on a rift): rifts never roll onto an outpost (Rifts.spot_ok), and an outpost founded on a hidden rift makes that rift tear open elsewhere for the same city (Rifts.move), so nothing gives the hidden rift away. tests/rift_outpost_test.gd checks it (100 games: 0 turns end with an outpost on a rift; Sköll's first hunt still about 25% per city). Income depends on the land (see Terrain below: plains +2 Food, forest +1/+1, hills +2 Industry), sight 2, claim their hex and the 6 around it as territory. Only a faction AT WAR with the owner can take an outpost: a unit stepping onto one nobody stands on takes it (at peace you just pass through; the player walking onto a peaceful faction's outpost declares war, with the two-click warning). Up to 3 founded per faction (captured ones can go over). The AI founds up to 2 before turn 10 and takes enemy outposts next to its units. A city that falls loses its outposts. Territory is a rule now (GameState.territory_owner); the view only colours it
- Small events (turn_events.gd): from turn 3-4, one every 2-3 turns (random), each at most once a game, player only. Every option costs something (Food, Industry, Favour, population, a coin flip, a unit standing watch for 2 turns ("guard_turns"), every AI's Trust ("trust_all"), or Food lost ("spoil", never below 0)); no option is free (Nathan's playtest: "post guards" was free, elk/traveller/merchants had free outs). Options you can't take are greyed out with the reason, and every event has an option that's always possible (tests/choices_test.gd checks both). Placeholder text
- Pop-ups: ruins and events go in GameState.pending_choices and are shown before any AI offer; the turn can't end until they're answered (GameState.is_waiting, can_act)
- Combat fix: beating a city's garrison no longer moves the attacker into the city
- Measured (tests/early_game_test.gd, 300 games, a test player that explores and builds): turns 1-10 with no decision 0 of 3000; pop-up on 33% of early turns; something affordable to build on 83%. Per game: 6.7 events, ruins searched 2.2 by you and 5.6 by the three AIs, you found 3.0 outposts, lost about 2 and took about 1.4 (before war and peace; since then outposts are only taken in a war: lost about 0.3-0.4 a game). Endings 225 survived / 49 city fell / 26 Daughter found
- Side effect to watch: the AIs expand too, so on turn 10 each AI has about 6 units (was 2.7). Against a player who doesn't expand, AI demands go from 0.5 to 1.25 a game

## Terrain, trading and tributaries: built (Nathan: "terrain first, then trading, cities become tributaries")

- Terrain (terrain.gd, GameState.terrain; standard 4X): plains, forest, hills, mountains, water, a new random map every game (weighted, clumped, always connected; AI cities on plains with passable neighbours). Forest and hills cost 2 moves, but a unit with all its moves can always take one step (armies still move 1 hex a turn; scouts are slowed). Mountains and water can't be crossed or stood on (Sköll ignores terrain). Defence: forest +1, hills +2, for units and for a city standing on them. Outposts earn by land: plains +2 Food, forest +1/+1, hills +2 Industry. Units find paths around obstacles (GameState.step_toward). Hovering a hex shows what its land does. Map ~53% plains, 21% forest, 14% hills, 6% mountains, 6% water
- Trading (diplomacy.gd; "Deal..." button per AI opens a panel; each deal uses the diplomatic action): demand 5 Food/Industry (as before); trade 5 of one for N of the other, where N = 4, +1 if they need what you give, -1 if they need what they give up, +1 at Trust 3+, -1 at Trust -3 or less (at least 1, at most what they have); gift 5 Food/Industry for Trust +1 (until Trust 5); at war, offer 6 Industry for peace (+30% chance, paid only if accepted). No trading at war. An AI short of one resource with enough of the other offers a trade (pop-up). Numbers TRADE_*, GIFT_*, PEACE_TRIBUTE* in diplomacy.gd
- Tributaries (game_state.gd "Taking cities"): a unit at war with a city's owner can attack the city from next to it: its Strength +2 for every other army of its own next to the city (siege support) against the whole city defence (garrison + walls + land + the Daughter). Win: the garrison dies, walls -1, and the city becomes the winner's TRIBUTARY (Faction.overlord); the attacker stays outside. Lose: the attacker dies. A tributary pays half its Industry income each turn to its overlord, their war ends, and neither can declare war on the other (nor be dragged into one by an alliance). Ways out: an AI tributary whose army is stronger than its overlord's rises up (20% a turn, then they're at war); the player rises up by attacking their overlord (two-click warning); taking your overlord's city frees you; if Sköll destroys the overlord's city its tributaries go free. Losing your city to an AI does NOT end the game (only Sköll destroying it does). AIs at war with 2+ armies send spare ones at the nearest enemy city and attack at 40%+ odds. Numbers TRIBUTE_SHARE, REBEL_CHANCE, SIEGE_SUPPORT, AI_SIEGE_ODDS in game_state.gd
- Closed borders (Nathan: "territory that needs politics to allow a border crossing, or war"; GameState.may_enter): at peace, units may not enter another faction's territory (its city and outposts and the 6 hexes around each). Allowed if at war, allied (an oath), with an open-borders agreement, or overlord/tributary. AIs keep borders closed to each other unless at war. The player stepping across a closed border declares war (two-click warning; the hex shows pink when a unit is selected). AI units path around closed territory. Open borders: a deal ("Ask for open borders", 40% + 10% per Trust, 10 turns, both ways; ends if war breaks out); an AI with Trust 1+ offers it (40% of its offers). Units left inside when borders close (peace made, agreement or oath ended, a city became a tributary) walk out to the nearest allowed hex at the end of the turn. Numbers BORDERS_LENGTH, RIVAL_BORDERS_TRUST in diplomacy.gd. Measured (border_test, 300 games): open-borders agreements 1.4 a game when the player asks often, AI offers 0.2, units walked out 0.3, never a unit inside closed borders at the end of a turn
- Measured (300 games each): trading about 3 trades and 1 gift a game, AIs offer 0.3 trades; cities taken about 0.07-0.1 a game (almost always an AI taking yours; you were a tributary on 0.4-0.6 turns a game); endings about 235-240 survived / 35-45 fell / 21-26 Daughter found. Siege odds: Strength 3 alone against defence 7 wins 30%, with two armies beside it 45-50%

## The Sun's Daughter: design in progress

Built so far: she chooses her protector at the Landing by Favour, judges her protector (step 1), can go rogue (step 2) and can be traded (step 3):
1. DONE (Step B): treating her well = how well you run your kingdom. Each turn her protector starves (a unit deserts): her Favour -2; every 3 turns in a row fed: +1. On turn 14 (Sköll arrives) she checks city defence 10+ and army Strength 6+: both +2, one 0, neither -3. From turn 10 she warns the player what she wants, and the line under the top bar shows progress. Only her protector is judged. Numbers at the top of sun_daughter.gd
2. DONE: going rogue. When her Favour toward her protector is 2 or less she walks out onto a hex next to the city (the player is warned at 4 or less) and the protector loses her bonuses. In the open she moves 1 hex a turn away from Sköll. Fog of war: Sköll only sees 2 hexes (Skoll.SIGHT_RANGE); if he spots her he hunts her instead of a city and sprints 3 hexes a turn (SPRINT; was 2); reaching her, he swallows her with no battle. Beyond his sight she is hidden from him. Moving a unit onto her tries to persuade her: chance = that faction's Favour x 10%; success, she shelters in its city. The rival's units go after her too. Numbers: LEAVE_FAVOUR etc. in sun_daughter.gd, SIGHT_RANGE and SPRINT in skoll.gd
3. DONE: trading her, from turn 10, by whoever shelters her (uses the player's diplomatic action). Her value to a faction is a price in Industry (-10 to 10): 6, +/-1 per point of her Favour toward them above/below 5, -3 once the howling starts or -6 once Sköll is loose, -3 more if he's hunting their city, -1 per 3 points their army is stronger than the giver's. Positive: they pay the giver (up to what they have). Negative: the giver pays them. Below -8: they refuse. Being traded costs her Favour toward the giver (-2). An AI sheltering her wants her gone (from turn 10) if her value to it is negative or Sköll is hunting its city; then each turn it has a 50% chance to offer her to the player, before any other offer: for nothing, or paying the player if her value is negative (Take her / Refuse pop-up). In tests an AI offers her in about 31% of games where it holds her. "Trade her" button opens a panel with each faction's price. Numbers: TRADE_* and FEAR_* in sun_daughter.gd

### Step A: economy with costs (standard 4X), in this order
1. DONE: several units per faction, built with Industry from the city (one unit per hex, destroyed units are gone, no free respawn; the rival builds too)
2. DONE: food upkeep: a city makes 2 Food a turn (+2 with the Daughter), every unit eats 1. Running out keeps Food at 0 and the unit farthest from the city deserts. The rival only builds units it can feed. Numbers are constants at the top of game_state.gd (CITY_FOOD, UNIT_FOOD_UPKEEP, UNIT_COST)
3. DONE: walls: a city improvement, 10 Industry per level, +4 defence each, up to 2 levels. The rival builds walls from turn 10. City defence is now the standard garrison + walls (+3 with the Daughter); stored Industry no longer counts. Numbers: WALL_COST, WALL_DEFENCE, MAX_WALLS in game_state.gd

## Diplomacy: agreed design

### War and peace (built; Nathan: "you forgot an important factor of 4X games")
- Every pair of factions starts at PEACE. At peace, units can't attack each other or take each other's outposts (GameState.at_war). Sköll is everyone's enemy regardless
- The player declares war by attacking a unit or walking onto an outpost of a faction they're at peace with (two clicks: the first warns). Doesn't use the diplomatic action. Their Trust -3, every other AI's -1 (warmonger). Attacking an ally also breaks the oath (Trust -5 instead, oathbreaker, her Favour -2)
- Peace: the player asks (the diplomatic action; the agreement button becomes "Peace N%" during a war). Chance 30% + 10% per Trust + 5% per point of army Strength you have over them, +20% once Sköll howls (5-95%). An AI at war offers peace (25% a turn) when it's weaker, afraid of Sköll, or the war has lasted 5+ turns. Peace: Trust +1 and a 5-turn truce in which they won't declare war
- An AI declares war on the player (10% a turn, never in a truce) if its Trust is -5 or less, or its army is 4+ stronger and its Trust under 2, or it's starving. An ally only does so when 4+ stronger or starving (25%), and that breaks its oath
- AIs fight each other too: an AI may declare war on another AI whose army is 4+ weaker (5% a turn); after 4 turns of war they make peace at 20% a turn (40% once Sköll howls), then a 5-turn truce. AIs still don't make alliances with each other
- Numbers: TRUST_DECLARED_WAR, TRUCE_TURNS, RIVAL_WAR_* etc. in diplomacy.gd; AI_WAR_* and AI_PEACE_* in game_state.gd
- Measured (tests/war_test.gd, 300 games, a player using diplomacy at random): per game the AIs declare war on you 1.3 times, on each other 0.6, peace is made 1.5 times; allies honoured their oath 0.48 and broke it 0.44 times; your ally was attacked (pop-up) 0.18 times. Your outposts taken per game fell from 2.0 to 0.4, AI-vs-AI fights from 6 to 1 (early_game_test). Endings 251 survived / 29 fell / 20 Daughter found

- Each faction has its own word for a sworn agreement (Faction.pact_word). Norse factions swear "oaths"; other pantheons later may use other words
- The player has a separate relationship with each AI faction (its own Trust, agreement, oathbreaker status, demands and broken-promise count): one Diplomacy object per AI in GameState.relations. AI factions don't do diplomacy with each other yet
- One diplomatic action per turn in total, shared across all factions (for now)
- Each AI keeps a Trust score toward the player (-10 to 10); what all factions remember (who kept or broke their word) is shown on screen
- Each turn, each AI has a 15% chance to make the player an offer, and at most one offer is shown per turn
- Steps, in order:
  1. DONE: oaths = DEFENSIVE ALLIANCES (Nathan's choice once war and peace existed). Propose a 10-turn oath, only at peace; the rival accepts by chance (50% + 10% per Trust, +20% once Sköll's howling starts, +10% if you have more units). If an AI declares war on the player, every AI ally declares war on the aggressor, unless the aggressor's army is 4+ stronger than the ally's: then the ally breaks its oath (ALLY_ABANDON_GAP). If an AI declares war on the player's ally, a pop-up asks the player: honour it (declare war on the aggressor: ally Trust +2, aggressor Trust -2, no warmonger penalty) or stay out (breaks the oath: Trust -5, oathbreaker, her Favour -2). Kept to the end: Trust +2. Breaking it by attacking the ally (two clicks): Trust -5, oathbreaker for 8 turns (no new oaths), the Sun's Daughter's Favour -2
  2. DONE: demands. Demand 5 Food or 5 Industry (uses the turn's diplomatic action). The rival pays with chance 50% + 10% per point of army Strength you have over it (5-95%), 0% if it doesn't have 5. Paying: Trust -1 (resentment, as is usual in 4X). Refusing: Trust -2. Demanding something the rival doesn't have gets a rude reply (lines in diplomacy.gd, CANT_PAY_REPLIES) and costs nothing: no action used, no Trust change
  3. DONE: the rival's own diplomacy. At the start of a player turn (15% chance per AI) it may propose an oath (if at peace and afraid of Sköll, weaker, or Trust 2+; never to an oathbreaker), demand 5 Food/Industry (if its army is 3+ Strength stronger), or, at war, offer peace. A pop-up asks Accept/Pay/Make peace or Refuse and blocks the turn until answered; answering doesn't use the player's own action. Accepting their oath Trust +1, refusing -1; paying their demand Trust +1, refusing -1. An ally may betray you by declaring war (see War and peace): it can attack that same turn, the panel counts its broken oaths, and its Favour with the Sun's Daughter drops by 2
- Numbers for all of this are constants at the top of diplomacy.gd

## Production queue: built (standard 4X, Nathan: "3 very important")

- Each city has a queue of up to 5 items (QUEUE_MAX; Faction.queue): army, scout, walls or a city building. The Build panel has a Queue button beside Army and Scout, the city buildings panel one beside each building, and a QUEUE section lists the items (each with an x), what the city works on ("Army 2/5, done in 2 turns") and Finish now
- How it works (GameState "Production queue", work_queue in end_turn): the Industry a turn brings in (after army upkeep and tribute) goes into the queue (Faction.production) instead of the store. When enough is in, the item is finished at the end of the turn (a new unit can move at once) and any extra goes on to the next item. An item that can't be finished right now (supply full, no free hex at the city) waits and the next one is worked on; if nothing in the queue can be finished, the Industry goes to the store as usual
- Industry in the queue belongs to the city: it can't pay for deals, demands or Sunna's requests. Taking an item out keeps it for the next item; taking the last one out (or finishing the last one) gives what's left back to the store
- Finish now: pays what the front item still needs from the store, 1 to 1 (a unit finished this way waits a turn, like buying at once). Buying at once from Build still works as before (Civ-style buying, without a premium, so the queue costs nothing extra: it saves clicks and lets you plan)
- Outposts aren't queued (they need a hex chosen on the map). The AIs keep buying at once (unchanged), so the AI and every simulation that doesn't queue play exactly as before (four_test, early_game_test, war_test: identical output on main and this branch, 20 seeded games)
- Measured (tests/queue_test.gd: every rule on a set-up game, then the same 300 seeds played by a player who buys at once and one who only queues): on turn 20 armies 3.4 -> 3.2, buildings 1.2 -> 1.3, Industry left in the store 11.6 -> 14.8 (the queue only spends each turn's income, so windfalls from the Landing and ruins sit in the store unless you press Finish now); devoured 73 -> 75 of 300; no army ever built over supply. tests/queue_screenshot.gd shows the Build panel with 5 items and the city buildings panel

## Zone of control: built (standard 4X, the classic rule)

- Every army holds the 6 hexes around it against factions at war with it (GameState.zoc_holder, in_enemy_zoc, zoc_blocks). Scouts hold nothing; Sköll and Sunna ignore it; at peace there is none
- Rule 1: stepping into an enemy army's zone ends the unit's move (enter_hex sets moves to 0). Armies have only 1 move, so this only slows scouts
- Rule 2: a unit already in an enemy zone can't step to another hex in an enemy zone: it can attack, step back out, or stay. This is what stops an army walking past an enemy army to the outpost or city behind it. Stepping into your own city is always allowed; attacking isn't limited
- Why both: the "stop when you move next to an enemy" rule alone (Civ 5/6 style) does nothing for 1-move armies, so the older Civ 1-3 rule 2 is added
- The AI obeys it: step_toward never picks a forbidden first step, and its outpost grabs, random steps and scouts skip forbidden hexes
- On screen: forbidden hexes next to your selected unit aren't highlighted; hovering one says why; an enemy army at war says it holds its zone; hovering a hex where a scout would stop says so. GameState.zoc_on is only for tests (to measure with the rule off)
- Measured (tests/zoc_test.gd): every rule on a set-up game; 300 games with the rule off -> on (same seeds, a player who wanders and sometimes attacks): your outposts lost 0.62 -> 0.53 a game, unit fights 7.8 -> 6.7, endings about the same (devoured 104 -> 103, Dawn 33 -> 34), no impossible states. A small change, as expected: armies rarely try to slip past each other today

## Unit health: built (Nathan's playtest after Part 1, from the backlog)

- Every unit has 10 HP (Unit.MAX_HP). Unit against unit, both sides take damage: COMBAT_DAMAGE (10) is shared out by Strength (each side deals 10 x its Strength / both Strengths), varied by +/-25%. Equal fresh units deal about 5 each (two fights to kill); Strength 6 against 3 deals 6.6 and takes 3.4. A unit at 0 HP dies; if the defender dies and the attacker lives, it moves in (never into a city)
- A wounded unit fights weaker: Strength x (0.5 + 0.5 x HP/10), never below 1 (Unit.fighting_strength); used attacking, defending, garrisoning a city and in sieges
- Healing (standard 4X): a unit that neither moved nor fought in a turn heals HEAL_REST 2, or HEAL_HOME 4 in its own territory (Unit.acted). AI units at 4 HP or less rest instead of attacking
- Since Part 2: attacks on cities and fights with Sköll use HP too (city HP, Sköll's wounds: see "Middle and late game")
- On screen: an HP bar under every unit (green to yellow to red); the tooltip shows HP, Strength while wounded, healing, and, next to your selected unit, the damage an attack would deal and take. Map tooltips sit above the hovered hex so its neighbours stay visible
- Measured (tests/health_test.gd): 1000-fight averages as above; 4 unit fights a game, half of them kill a unit; HP never out of range

## Army limits: built (Nathan's playtest: 17 units, Strength 100 by turn 20; "a strong player should have roughly 6-8 units at turn 20, and Sköll should still be a real threat")

- Supply (standard 4X): at most SUPPLY_BASE (2) + population armies, +2 with a Barracks (GameState.supply_cap). Scouts don't count. The Army button shows armies/supply and greys out when full
