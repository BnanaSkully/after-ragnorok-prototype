# After Ragnarök — Concept Art Prompts

Nov 15, 2026 · @Nathan

## How to use this

- **Before starting the trial:** read these over and change anything that doesn't feel like your game. The trial clock only starts once you sign up.
- **Model:** Nano Banana Pro or GPT Image for these. Aspect ratio 16:9 unless a shot says otherwise.
- **Every prompt = style block + shot prompt.** Paste the style block first, then the shot. That's what makes the images look like one game.
- **Order:** core shots 1–4 first. Only move to 5–8 once the core look feels right.
- **Variations:** run each shot 3–4 times as is, then once or twice with a single change (camera closer, more fog, warmer light). Change one thing at a time so you learn what each word does.
- **Save:** keep the best 1–2 of each shot, plus anything surprising. Name them by shot number (e.g. 02-settlement-b).
- **No game names in prompts.** Describing the qualities gets you something original; naming existing games gets you a copy of their look.
- **Cancel the auto-renewal right after signing up**, if Higgsfield lets you keep the trial until it ends.

## Style block (paste before every prompt)

```
Concept art for a turn-based strategy game. Stylised low-poly 3D hexagonal tile map seen from a tilted high camera. A newborn world after Ragnarök, rising from a grey sea, stitched together from shards of dead Norse realms. Simple faceted shapes, rich atmosphere. Cold moonlight from the upper left, low volumetric fog, deep soft shadows. Desaturated palette of pale greys, slate and cold blue-white. Warm orange firelight and colour appear only where people live. Bleak, survival mood with quiet beauty. High detail, painterly finish.
```

Negatives (add at the end, or in the negative field if the tool has one):

```
No text, no UI, no watermark, no logos, no modern objects, no photorealistic faces, no bright saturated fantasy colours, no cartoon style.
```

Palette to check results against: sea #1C2830, raw land #7F898E, ice #B7CBD6, black rock #2E2724, lava #E2672A, growth #5D8A50, firelight #F0A35E, banner red #A8322D.

## Core shots

### 1. The map (hero shot)

What it answers: does the whole look work at gameplay distance?

```
Wide view of a small hex map island. Top left: a raised shard of pale blue glacier ice with frozen mist. Top right: tall black basalt hexes split by glowing lava cracks, embers drifting. Edges: low dark grey sea tiles. Middle: misty grey unclaimed land, flat and unfinished. Centre: one small settled region where the hexes have turned green, with a wooden longhouse, firelight and a red banner. Bottom right: ash-grey hexes sinking and crumbling, colour draining out.
```

Variations: camera lower and closer; night versus grey dawn; heavier fog over the unclaimed land.

### 2. A settlement at night

What it answers: what does "colour lives where people live" feel like up close?

```
Close view of three or four hex tiles around a small Norse survivor settlement at night. Turf-roofed longhouses built from salvaged wreckage, a central fire pit, red cloth banners, tiny figures huddled around warm orange light. Green moss and young grass spreading on the settled hexes, ending abruptly where cold grey misty land begins. Moonlight on the fog beyond.
```

Variations: in a blizzard; under threat with distant red glow on the horizon; at dawn.

### 3. Niflheim shard

What it answers: what does an ice shard look like as playable terrain?

```
A cluster of raised hex tiles made of ancient blue-white glacier ice, cracked and faceted, frozen mist pooling between them. Half-buried in the ice: the ribs of a giant creature and a broken stone rune pillar. Cold, silent, beautiful and dangerous. Grey sea at the edges.
```

Variations: with a small outpost of survivors on it; one hex thawing into meltwater.

### 4. Muspelheim shard

What it answers: what does a fire shard look like next to the cold world?

```
A cluster of the tallest hex tiles: black basalt columns split by glowing orange lava seams, ash and embers rising, heat haze. Where it meets the grey misty land and the sea, steam rises. Rich but deadly: veins of dark metal ore in the rock. The only warm natural light on the map.
```

Variations: lava cooled to dull red after many turns; a mine camp built at the edge.

## Extra shots (once the core look is right)

### 5. The Doom arrives

```
The same hex map late in the game. From one edge, the world is dying: hexes turning to ash and sinking into darkness, colour draining out of everything it touches, fog turning black. In the dark sky above, the vast shadow of a giant wolf swallowing the light. One settlement's firelight still burning against it.
```

### 6. The Crossing (key art, 21:9 or 16:9)

```
The World Tree Yggdrasil torn from its roots, drifting through a dark starless void like an ark, its branches crowded with tiny lights of survivor camps. Behind it, the old worlds burning. Ahead, faint grey land rising from a misty sea. Epic scale, quiet and desperate.
```

### 7. Sunken Midgard shard

```
A cluster of low marshland hex tiles where the old human world drowned: half-submerged stone halls, a broken longship mast, reeds and black water, will-o-wisp lights in the mist. Melancholy and haunted.
```

### 8. Mood triptych (3:1)

```
Three panels showing the same single hex tile in three states, left to right: unclaimed (pale grey rock, fog, no colour); claimed (green growth, a small hut, warm firelight, red banner); Doom-drained (grey ash, cracked and sinking, colour gone, black fog). Identical camera angle in each panel.
```

This last one is the most useful reference for later game art, so give it a few extra runs.

## Picking and bringing results back

- **Judge against the style tile, not against "is it pretty".** Does it read as one game? Is colour only where people live? Could it work as a game view, not just a painting?
- **Bring back the best 1–2 per shot**, plus anything that surprised you. We'll note which words worked and update the style block before the next batch.
- **Reference only.** These are for direction. Final in-game art should be human-made, a kit, or heavily reworked, so nothing AI-generated ships in the game or its marketing without a decision about Steam's disclosure.
- **Not covered yet:** faction and character studies. Leave them until the prototype's two factions are settled.
